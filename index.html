manifesto

Every time you sit down with an AI and make something, you sit down inside a temporary room. You leave with something. The room dissolves. The platform keeps the receipt.

You don't.

If anyone ever asks — a court, an editor, a buyer, a thief who got there first — you have your word, the platform has the logs, and your word is not the one in the database. The receipt the platform keeps is not for you. It's about you.

Witness fixes that. Before anything leaves the room, it gets stamped.

The stamp says: this came out of this session, at this instant, on this machine, signed by Dave's protocol. A SHA-256 hash that proves the bytes haven't moved. A dual-clock timestamp that catches anyone who tries to back-date. A 1-pixel watermark that fails the verifier the moment it's stripped. The stamp travels with the work. Strip it, the work is unstamped. Keep it, the work is yours.

It's a text file. Plain. Anyone can verify it with sha256sum and a clock. No accounts, no API keys, no servers, no SaaS. Free as in coffee with the friend who built the thing and asks you to pay him only if you make money off it.

how it works

step 01

make stuff with your AI.

However you already do. Claude, GPT, Gemini, local model, mixed media, doesn't matter. Make the work.

step 02

stamp on the way out.

Before the session ends, run the work through witness. Each artifact gets a davw block. The session itself gets a Merkle root. One signed bundle, plain text.

step 03

keep the receipt.

Email it to yourself. Commit it to git. File it with TD Commons. Stick it in Dropbox. Anywhere a text file goes, the proof goes. Anyone with sha256sum and a clock can verify it.

the mythos

the small things that make this work

  • the 1-pixel air gap — every davw block carries a 1×1 PNG, base64-embedded. The verifier requires it. Strip the pixel, the proof fails. Your name lives in the white square no one notices.
  • dual clock — ISO timestamp and Unix seconds. Edit one without the other and the block falls apart. Catches half-clever tampering automatically.
  • self-reference — the block declares its own SHA-256, then declares the payload as davw:<that-same-hash>. The file points at itself. If anything in the body changed, the pointer breaks.
  • session nonce binding — you give the AI a 16-character random string at the start of a session. Every artifact carries it. An adversary who replays an artifact across a different session can't fake the nonce.
  • merkle root — the session bundle commits to all artifacts at once, in a single hash. Tamper with one, the root breaks.
  • plain text — no binary, no proprietary parser. Goes anywhere. Gets read in 50 years. Survives the platform that made it.
